## Design Context

### Users

Planes is for non-programmers as well as programmers. Public demonstrations
should let a visitor encounter the language through play, observation, and
direct manipulation before they need to read or understand source code. The
job is to make computation, movement, interaction, effects, and provenance
feel tangible and inspectable.

### Brand Personality

Calm, curious, and rigorous. Planes should feel like a small world that is
alive enough to explore, but precise enough to trust. Its visual language
should make technical truthfulness welcoming rather than clinical.

### Aesthetic Direction

Garden is the style anchor: paper ground, graphite ink, clay/slate/amber/
blue/teal/violet accents, Red Hat Display and Martian Mono, restrained framed
panels, and a canvas-led composition. Reuse the generated Planes identity
assets and the self-hosted fonts from `identity/`; do not invent a separate
brand or rely on remote design assets. `A Crossing` is grounded in the
canonical Ala Eriri corpus: its visual world is a South Atlantic volcanic
archipelago of cord-anchored terraces, hydrofoils, swells, fog catchers, and
low coastal landings. Its atmosphere is sunlit, contemporary Atlantic and
Caribbean-island in spirit: saturated sea, lush slopes, market colour,
community music, and joyful daily life alongside real weather and civic
stakes. It must feel like a sibling to Garden, not a dashboard, a generic
city-management game, or a dark medieval European port.

### Design Principles

1. Make the running world primary; controls and explanations support it.
2. Treat every selectable object as an invitation to inspect real program
   output and provenance.
3. Let play create curiosity, then reveal source and explanation progressively.
4. Randomness may choose a visible, replayable seed; running a chosen seed
   remains deterministic.
5. Never confuse a simulated world decision with a real host effect.
6. A successor to Garden must meet or exceed its visual richness and authored
   atmosphere; technical interaction prototypes are never the visual bar.
